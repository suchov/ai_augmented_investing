# KASPI — AI Analyst Summary

*Source: KASPI_ProResearch.pdf.pdf · via claude CLI (opus)*

## Business

**How it earns money.** Kaspi.kz runs a three-segment "Super App" in Kazakhstan: **Payments** (transaction fees on a payments network used by ~78% of the country's adults), **Marketplace** (commissions/GMV take on ~200,000 merchants, plus e-commerce and delivery), and **Fintech** (consumer lending — the P&L's real profit engine, funded largely by deposits). LTM revenue KZT 4.30T with 69.2% gross margin and 54% operating margin (KZT 2.33T operating profit). The report gives **no segment-level revenue or profit split** — a meaningful gap, since the economics of a payments toll booth and a subprime-adjacent consumer lender are entirely different businesses. Newer monetization: value-added services (ads, logistics, analytics), +73% YoY in Q1 2026. Recently added Türkiye via **Hepsiburada** and **Rabobank A.Ş.** acquisitions.

**Moat.** Genuine and multi-sourced, at least domestically:
- **Network effects** — 15.7M users, 10.1M DAU, 68.7% DAU/MAU (unusually high engagement), two-sided with ~200k merchants.
- **Switching costs / habit** — 15 purchases per user per quarter, +44% YoY.
- **Brand** — synonymous with mobile payments in Kazakhstan.
- **Data** — proprietary credit scoring built on payments + marketplace transaction data, cited as driving high portfolio yields with low defaults. This is the most durable piece, and also the least verifiable from this report (no NPL, cost-of-risk, or loan-book disclosure).
- **Cost advantage** — asset-light marketplace, low capex.

The honest caveat: this is a moat around a single country of ~20M people that is already ~78% penetrated. The moat protects the existing profit pool; it does not obviously travel to Türkiye.

**Competitors.** **Not covered in report** — no domestic competitor is named. For Türkiye the report only says "established local players and international tech giants," again unnamed. That is a real omission for an ownership decision.

## Financials

**Growth (FY2022 → FY2025, 3 years):**
| Metric | 2022 | 2025 | CAGR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | KZT 1.269T | KZT 4.043T | **~47%** |
| Net income | KZT 585B | KZT 1.073T | **~22%** |
| Diluted EPS | 3,016 | 5,592 | **~23%** |
| Cash from operations | KZT 1.021T | KZT 674B | **~ –13%** |

The gap between those lines is the whole story. Net margin has collapsed from **46.1% (2022) → 26.5% (2025) → 24.9% (LTM)**. Net income growth is **–0.16%** LTM and Q1 2026 net income actually *fell* (KZT 249.4B vs 252.1B) despite revenue +31%. Adjusted EBITDA +9% on revenue +31% says the same thing.

**FCF CAGR:** effectively **negative**. The "Levered Free Cash Flow" row is corrupted (1.0 / 1.1 / 0.5 / 0.5 / 0.7), but EV/FCF of 25.0 → **275.7** → 44.0 across 2023–2025 and CFO falling from KZT 1.02T to KZT 674B confirms it. Quarterly CFO swings violently negative (–124.7B, –111.4B). This is normal for a lender — loans originated run through operating cash flow — but it means the headline **8.92% FCF yield is not a distributable-cash number** and the 2025 table's own 4.74% contradicts it.

**Returns on capital.** LTM **ROE 48.7%**, ROI 69.7%, ROA 10.5%. Excellent by any standard. But note the report's narrative claims "~80% multi-year ROE" while the actual LTM figure is 48.7% — returns are compressing fast as equity compounds (KZT 826B → 2,775B in three years) faster than earnings. True ROIC is **Not covered in report**, and for a deposit-funded lender ROE is a weak proxy for it.

**Balance sheet.** Strong on the face of it. Total debt only **KZT 329B** against KZT 2,775B equity (~12% debt/equity) and falling from KZT 603B a year ago. Equity/assets of 25% (2,775B / 11,098B) is healthy for a bank-like entity. Current assets KZT 8.57T vs current liabilities KZT 1.21T. But **KZT 8.32T of total liabilities is essentially all non-debt** — overwhelmingly customer deposits — and the report tells you nothing about deposit cost, stickiness, or the loan book's credit quality. The report itself flags "higher funding costs in Kazakhstan." For a lender, that is the balance sheet question, and it is unanswered here.

**Dividend red flag.** Yield by year: 7.74% (2023) → 5.92% (2024) → **0.01% (2025)** → 4.06% (2026). The report calls this "consistent dividend payments" and cites a "1 year" growth streak. Those two things cannot both be true — 2025 looks like a skipped or near-zero payout.

## Risks

**Regulatory.** The most concrete near-term threat, and it hits earnings directly:
- Corporate tax rate potentially rising **toward 25%**
- New **10% tax on government securities revenue** (relevant given a bank's securities book)
- **Consumer loan interest rate cap, currently 56%** — any tightening cuts straight into the Fintech segment's yield, which is where the profit is
- Kazakhstan inflation **10.4%** (May 2026) vs a 5% target → higher funding costs
- December 2024 **securities class action** alleging undisclosed post-2022 Russian-entity exposure and undisclosed related-party transactions. Kaspi denies it. Related-party allegations at a company that is effectively national financial infrastructure deserve more than a shrug.
- **Secondary sanctions exposure** via geopolitical position between Russia and the West.

**AI / technological disruption.** **Not covered in report.** The only technology content is offensive (Kaspi Alaqan palm-scanning payments) and the credit-scoring data advantage. No discussion of AI as a threat to the underwriting moat or to the marketplace.

**Concentration.** Not customer or supplier — it's **country**. One market, one currency (KZT depreciation risk), one regulator, one oil-linked macro. The report also names **cybersecurity/platform risk** as elevated because Kaspi *is* the payment rails for millions; one breach or extended outage is existential in a way it isn't for a diversified bank.

**Other red flags.**
- **Growth runway is largely spent domestically** (~78% adult penetration), making the risky Türkiye expansion mandatory rather than optional.
- FY2026 consensus EPS **cut 8.4%** over 12 months ($12.60 → $11.54); 90-day revisions ↑0 / ↓1.
- Q1 2026 revenue fell sequentially (KZT 1,065B vs 1,131B in Q4 2025).
- **Strong Sell** on both moving averages and technical indicators; –17.3% two-year total return (11.9th percentile).
- The PEG of –19.2 is a data artifact, not a signal — ignore it; the underlying fact it reflects (near-zero earnings growth) is what matters.
- **Report internal inconsistencies**: fair value stated as both **$122.8 (42.5% upside)** and **$105.04 (32.53% upside)**; FCF yield 8.92% vs 4.74%; "analyst upside 7.29%" vs a $96.85 median target on an $86.5 price (~12%). Treat these numbers as loosely maintained.

## Valuation

**The report's estimate.** Two conflicting figures. The **$122.8 / +42.5%** pair is internally consistent with today's $86.5 price; the **$105.04 / +32.53%** pair is consistent with the $78.58 price quoted in the June 11 news item — i.e. it's the stale one. Median analyst target **$96.85** (+12%), range $85.47–$121.03. Consensus **4 Buy / 3 Hold / 0 Sell**; Citi Buy, target trimmed $99 → $97.

**Multiples.** P/E 7.29 LTM, 7.01 forward, 5.11 by FY28. EV/EBITDA **2.57**. P/B 2.91. Market cap $16.4B on KZT 1.07T (~$2B) of net income.

**Cheap, fair, or rich?** *Statistically* cheap — but the market is not being stupid, and the multiple has been de-rating for a reason. Look at what actually happened: cap fell from $18.0B (2024) to $14.9B (2025) while revenue rose 53%. P/E went 10.1 → 9.71 → 6.86. The market re-rated Kaspi from a compounder to a cyclical single-country lender the moment earnings growth stopped. At 7x with **0% earnings growth**, ~10% domestic inflation, a pending tax increase, a rate cap, and litigation over sanctions disclosure, the stock is arguably **fair, not cheap**. A 7x P/E on a Kazakhstan-domiciled consumer lender is a normal price, not a gift. The EV/EBITDA of 2.57 should be discounted heavily — EV is a poor measure for a deposit-funded balance sheet.

**Bull — what the market may be missing.** 2026 is an investment trough, not a broken model. If revenue is compounding 20–24% and the margin drag is Türkiye integration spend plus a temporary funding-cost spike, then earnings re-accelerate (+16% FY27, +19% FY28) and you own a 68.7% DAU/MAU monopoly at 5x forward earnings with a 4% dividend and beta of 0.09. Value-added services +73% is genuinely high-margin, underpenetrated B2B revenue. The 100M-customer regional TAM is a sixfold optionality.

**Bear — what could go wrong.** The margin compression is structural, not cyclical: the domestic market is saturated, incremental revenue comes from lower-margin e-commerce GMV and a lower-margin Turkish business, funding costs stay elevated, and the rate cap tightens. Under that reading, Kaspi is a mature ~20%-net-margin lender growing earnings at low single digits, and 7x is the *right* multiple — with Türkiye actively destroying value (which the report says analysts may already be pricing in). Add tail risk from the sanctions litigation and from KZT depreciation, which hits USD-denominated ADR returns regardless of KZT-reported results.

## Verdict

Yes — a patient value investor should dig deeper, but with the specific skepticism that this is a *cheap single-country lender with a great franchise*, not a discounted compounder. The franchise quality is real and rare: 78% adult penetration, 68.7% DAU/MAU, 48.7% ROE, and a data-driven underwriting advantage that a competitor cannot buy. What is missing is any evidence that the profit engine still compounds. Revenue grew 53% and net income grew **–0.16%**; operating cash flow shrank ~13% annually over three years while revenue grew ~47%. That divergence is either the best entry point you'll get or the beginning of a permanent de-rating, and this report does not contain enough to tell you which.

**The single most important thing to verify next: a segment-level bridge from +31% revenue growth to ~0% net income growth in Q1 2026.** Specifically, how much of the revenue increase is low-margin consolidated Hepsiburada/Türkiye GMV being grossed up into the top line (which would mechanically crush margins without any deterioration in the core), versus rising cost of funds and rising cost of risk in the Kazakh Fintech loan book (which would be genuine impairment of the earnings power). Pull the segment disclosures, the net interest margin trend, and cost-of-risk/NPL data from the actual filings — the Fintech segment is where the profit lives, and this report tells you nothing about its credit quality. Second priority: the substance of the December 2024 related-party and sanctions-exposure allegations, since a governance problem at a company that *is* the national payment rails is not a discount you can underwrite.
